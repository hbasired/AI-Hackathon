Paper Trail: honest references,
with an AI research assistant

Find, organise, cite and trace every source. AI suggests. Plain code checks. The student decides.

Reference managers organise…

Zotero and Mendeley keep sources tidy, but never check that a reference is real or says what you claim.

…AI is fast, and invents

Chatbots write references that look right: a real author on the wrong paper, a DOI that does not exist, a quote the paper never contained.

…and the student is blamed

A failed assignment or an integrity case, for a mistake no tool caught.

It happens in real life. Mata v. Avianca (S.D.N.Y., 22 June 2023): two lawyers and their firm were fined $5,000 for a brief citing cases ChatGPT invented.

Research measures it. Walters & Wilder (2023), Scientific Reports: 55% of GPT-3.5's citations and 18% of GPT-4's were fabricated.

The rule

A source can be cited only when three keys have turned

1

Found

A scholarly registry returned the record: Crossref, OpenAlex, DataCite or doi.org. Never the AI's memory.

→
2

Checked

Plain code compared title, authors, year and DOI, asked a second registry, and looked for a retraction.

→
3

Approved

The student turned the key, or kept a flagged source with a written reason that travels with it.

AI holds none of the keys. It can suggest searches, point at sentences and argue against you. It cannot create a reference, decide a label or write the assignment.

Reference labels (Checker)

✓ Confirmed 1 One registry only ≠ Details differ ⇄ Wrong paper ✕ Retracted ? Not found … Could not check

Exports print them as Verified, Needs checking, Needs fix, Mismatch, Retracted, Not found and Not checked.

Claim labels (Quote guard)

✓ Verified Reported, not checked Disputed

Green only means proven. A registry that did not answer gives "Could not check", never a pass. We never say "fake": we say where we looked.

Inside the Checker: one reference

Every label comes from plain code, with a receipt

1 · Parse

Any style: APA, Harvard, MLA, IEEE, Vancouver, BibTeX, RIS, a bare DOI or a web address.

2 · Look up

With a DOI: Crossref, then DataCite, then doi.org. Without: title search in Crossref and OpenAlex.

3 · Compare

Title, authors, year and DOI against the record, field by field.

4 · Second registry

OpenAlex must hold the same work. A source never vouches for itself.

5 · Retractions

The publisher's notice in Crossref, plus the Retraction Watch index shipped with the site (62,611 retractions).

6 · Label + receipt

Each registry asked, and what it answered. Field by field: you wrote, the registry has.

SituationLabel
Two registries agree, every field matches✓ Confirmed
Real paper, one detail wrong (for example the year)≠ Details differ: fix offered
DOI exists but has another title, or none of the given authors⇄ Wrong paper
Publisher or Retraction Watch notice✕ Retracted
No registry has it; DOI does not exist? Not found, where we looked
A registry did not answer… Could not check, never a pass

The student decides

A suggested fix lists each change ("you wrote 1981, the registry has 1979") with Accept the fix or Keep mine. An accepted fix is checked again. Nothing is changed, swapped or deleted silently.

No guessing

A title alone is never Confirmed: works can share a title. A web page is labelled as a web source, not checked in a scholarly database. A paper with no DOI is searched by title, never called fake.

Tracing a claim to its source

The exact sentence, or an honest "not there"

1 · The claim

The student types the sentence they want to write.

2 · Closest sentences

Found by shared words and, on request, by meaning (an on-device model that only orders sentences).

3 · Rule guards

Is it word for word? Do the numbers match? The direction (rise or fall)? The scope ("all" or "some")?

4 · Support score

An on-device model scores support, neutral and contradiction against a 90% line.

5 · judge()

Plain code gives the label. A high score never overrides a failed guard.

The model we fine-tuned

DeBERTa-v3-xsmall, fine-tuned on SciFact (real scientific claims checked against abstracts). It runs in the browser via Transformers.js, so claims are not sent anywhere.

Wrong "contradicts" calls on 1,353 held-out SciFact pairs: 165 → 11.

If the site cannot serve it, the original model loads from Hugging Face instead. Quotations are never confirmed unless the exact words are in the cited source.

Limits we say out loud

  • Claim numbers are a development set: the rules were tuned on it. They are not hold-out results.
  • Claims are compared with abstracts unless the student adds the PDF. Registries can be incomplete, and "Not found" is not proof of fabrication.
  • The framework mapping is our own, not a certification. The evidence log detects edits; it is not tamper-proof.
